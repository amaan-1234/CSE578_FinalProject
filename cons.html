Orbital Debris Distribution

Earth surrounded by space objects across three orbital zones: LEO, MEO, and GEO. Each dot is a satellite, rocket body, or debris fragment traveling at 17,500 mph.

Early Buildup (2000-2008)

Orbital traffic was manageable. LEO contained the most objects—operational satellites, spent rocket stages, and collision fragments.

Accelerating Congestion (2009-2016)

Density increased sharply. The 2009 Iridium-Cosmos collision created over 2,000 debris fragments. Each collision exponentially increases the Kessler Syndrome risk—cascading collisions rendering orbits unusable.

Critical Mass (2017-2025)

Mega-constellations transformed orbital space. The dramatic LEO increase includes operational satellites and debris. At orbital velocities, even paint chips are deadly projectiles.

Unsustainable Trajectory

Orbital environment at critical capacity. Unlike oceans, orbital debris persists for decades. Without aggressive mitigation, we risk making certain orbits permanently unusable.
