Near-Earth Objects: Hazard Classification

This bar graph shows the amount of Near-Earth objects detected through the development of technological capability over time, from the year 2008 to 2022.

The rising bars in the graph demonstrate how our capability to detect NEOs as they approach the Earth has improved, allowing for earlier identification of NEOs which are potential threats to our planet and thus become the first step in mitigating risk to Earth.

Enhanced detection systems and space-based telescopes have exponentially increased our awareness of objects that could pose a threat, giving us more time to develop mitigation strategies.

Near-Earth Objects: Size vs Distance Analysis

This scatterplot shows the distances that asteroid flybys have been from Earth versus their brightness (magnitude).

The chart provides a comprehensive view of all the objects that have recently been flying near Earth and the varying degrees of danger they present.

Objects closer to Earth and with higher brightness (lower magnitude values) represent more significant potential threats. This visualization helps scientists prioritize which objects require continued monitoring and potential defensive action.

Government vs Private (1957-2020)

Government (green) vs Private (orange) launches across 63 years. Watch the balance shift from government monopoly to commercial dominance.

Government Dominance

The green stream dominates—decades of government monopoly. The tiny orange sliver shows minimal private activity. Space required nation-state resources.

Private Sector Emerges

The orange stream expands dramatically. SpaceX, Rocket Lab, and others develop reusable rockets. Competition drives innovation—costs plummet, frequency increases.

Historic Crossover

Orange dominates. Private launches outnumber government for the first time. Government agencies become customers—NASA buys from SpaceX, Pentagon contracts commercial providers.

Democratized Space

From green government monopoly to orange commercial industry. Lower costs through reusability, higher frequency through competition—space access democratized.

Mission Success Shield

Concentric rings represent decades of mission success rates, color-coded: red (low) to yellow (moderate) to green (high). Watch the shield strengthen as rings glow greener.

Experimental Era (1960s)

The innermost ring shows harsh reality—more failures than successes. Rockets exploded, satellites failed. Learning through trial and error where success was uncertain.

Building Reliability (1970s)

Ring glows warmer. Experience improved success rates—better quality control, redundant systems. Apollo, Skylab, Voyager missions strengthened the shield.

Operational Maturity (1990s)

Ring glows green—dramatic improvement. Space missions became remarkably reliable. Computer simulations, advanced materials, and decades of experience made success expected, not hoped for.

Near-Perfect Reliability (2010s-2020s)

Outermost ring shines brightest green—near-perfect success rates. Each decade builds on the last. The shield metaphor is literal: humanity's knowledge protects missions from past mistakes.

External Threats

Red dots outside the shield—Near-Earth Objects (asteroids/comets). Our success shield protects against human error, but cosmic hazards remain. We've mastered spaceflight technology; nature remains unpredictable.

Foundation for the Future

From uncertain yellows/oranges (1960s) to brilliant greens (2010s-2020s). A protective shield of knowledge and reliability enabling permanent space stations, lunar bases, and Mars missions.
